Abstract

Tirzepatide, a dual glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) receptor agonist approved in 2022, has become a widely used pharmacological alternative to bariatric surgery for weight management, in addition to improving glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes. Its effects on multiple organs raise concerns for surgeons and anaesthesiologists, mainly delayed gastric emptying, yet evidence on tirzepatide-specific perioperative complications is fragmented and has not been synthesised within a single framework.
This systematic review assessed the available data on surgical and perioperative complications linked to tirzepatide use: nausea and vomiting, pancreatitis, cholecystitis, cholelithiasis, aspiration, residual gastric content, ileus, wound dehiscence and medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC).
The review follows PRISMA 2020. PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Cochrane and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched from 2022 to 2026 for randomised controlled trials and observational studies in adults receiving tirzepatide in relation to surgical complications. Study quality was assessed with ROBINS-I, and data were synthesised with the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) approach.
Residual gastric content was consistently more frequent in patients exposed to tirzepatide or GLP-1 agonists, reaching statistical significance in the studies assessing it. However, no statistically significant increase in aspiration or aspiration pneumonia was found in eight observational studies. Nausea and vomiting were consistently more common with tirzepatide, particularly in pharmacovigilance reports. Data on acute pancreatitis, gallbladder and biliary disease, ileus and wound dehiscence were heterogeneous, with several studies favouring the exposed group.
Contrary to the prevailing practice of withholding tirzepatide before surgery, perioperative care should be tailored to patients’ symptoms and risk factors. Prospective studies focused specifically on tirzepatide are urgently needed.
